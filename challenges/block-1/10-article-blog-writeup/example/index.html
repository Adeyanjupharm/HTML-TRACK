Why Semantic HTML Actually Matters

Before this week, I thought a div with a class name was basically the same thing as a properly labeled semantic element — after all, they both just wrap content, right? Working through the Block 1 challenges changed my mind completely. When I rebuilt my bio page using header, main, and footer instead of divs, I noticed my code got easier to read at a glance, even without any styling. I didn't need to guess what each section was for because the tag itself told me.

The bigger surprise was learning how much semantic markup affects people using screen readers. A div means nothing to assistive technology, but a nav, a main, or a footer gives real landmarks that let someone skip straight to the content they care about. That reframed HTML for me: it's not just about how a page looks, it's about how the underlying structure communicates meaning to browsers, search engines, and real people navigating in ways I hadn't considered before. I'm going into Block 2 much more aware of that responsibility.
